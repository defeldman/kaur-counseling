[H1] ADHD & Late-Stage Diagnosis.
[P] A diagnosis found in adulthood re-reads an entire life.
[P] An ADHD diagnosis arriving later in life doesn't only name the present. It offers a new language for the past. The relief it can bring is real, and so is the grief that travels beside it. My work is to hold both, and to help you build a life that fits the mind you actually have.
[H2] ADD in women looks different
[P] ADHD in women is often missed, minimized, or explained away as anxiety, laziness, or not trying hard enough. Many women become experts at compensating until the strategies stop working.
[H2] When the diagnosis arrives later
[P] A late diagnosis can bring relief and grief at the same time. It can explain the unfinished projects, the exhaustion, and the sense that everyone else received an instruction manual you never got.
[H2] How it shows up
[P] Relationships · Rejection sensitivity can make a pause, a tone, or a missed text feel enormous.
[P] Work & career · You may be capable and praised while privately relying on urgency, overwork, and last-minute adrenaline.
[P] School & study · You were bright enough to coast, until the structure became more complex than motivation could carry.
[P] Self-esteem · A running inner monologue can turn ordinary friction into evidence that you are failing.
[P] Body & food · Forgetting to eat, sleep, or notice your own needs can become part of the rhythm.
[P] Sex & intimacy · Being distracted, flooded, or disconnected from your body can make closeness harder to access.
[H2] The grief, and the broken trust in yourself
[P] There may be grief for the years you spent blaming yourself, and anger at how hard you had to work to look okay. Part of our work is rebuilding trust with the person who has been carrying all of this.
[H2] How we work with it
[P] A thousand things at once · We practice turning a storm into one next thing.
[P] Hyperfocus ↔ overwhelm · We build a rhythm that does not depend on crisis.
[P] Forgetfulness about what you love · We make room for the interests and people that restore you.
[P] Time warps and vanishes · We externalize time without turning your life into a punishment.
[P] The exhaustion of masking · We notice where performing has replaced being.
[P] The intention-action gap · We separate knowing what to do from having the support to do it.
[P] You are not a problem to be fixed. Your brain is asking for a different kind of environment.
